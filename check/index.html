Music Exposure Check

Ten questions, about two minutes. You see which music licenses probably apply to you, where you may be paying twice, and where you may have a gap. The check uses US rules for now: it is for venues in the United States (where we work).

Runs entirely in your browser. Nothing is stored or sent. General information only, not legal advice.

Why it matters

Playing music in a business counts as a public performance. In the US, songwriters are paid through performing rights organizations (PROs), mainly ASCAP, BMI, SESAC and GMR. Each one licenses only its own members’ songs, so paying one does not cover the others.

  • $4,500a year on average: what the National Restaurant Association says its members pay to license music.
  • ~200%rise in per-hotel PRO fees 2021 to 2025 at one unnamed global chain, cited by the American Hotel & Lodging Association.

Where a song is played without the right license, US law allows statutory damages of $750 to $30,000 per work, and up to $150,000 if a court finds the infringement willful.

Sources: Bloomberg via Indianapolis Business Journal, “Restaurants, bars say they’re getting squeezed by rising music licensing costs” (July 9, 2025); 17 U.S.C. §504(c), statutory damages.
