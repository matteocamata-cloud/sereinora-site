What is the music in your venue really costing you?

Ten questions, two minutes. See which licenses probably apply, where you may be paying twice, and where you may be exposed.

Runs entirely in your browser. Nothing is stored or sent. General information only, not legal advice.

Why it matters

Playing recorded or live music in a business is a public performance. Songwriters’ rights are licensed by performing rights organizations (PROs), mainly ASCAP, BMI, SESAC and GMR, and one PRO’s license does not cover another’s songs.

  • $4,500a year on average: what the National Restaurant Association says its members pay to license music.
  • ~200%rise in per-hotel PRO fees 2021 to 2025 at one unnamed global chain, cited by the American Hotel & Lodging Association.
  • $30,000the usual ceiling on statutory damages per infringed work (the floor is $750). Up to $150,000 per work if willful.

Sources: Bloomberg via Indianapolis Business Journal, “Restaurants, bars say they’re getting squeezed by rising music licensing costs” (July 9, 2025); 17 U.S.C. §504(c), statutory damages.
