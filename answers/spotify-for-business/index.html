Can I play Spotify in my restaurant or bar?

Not with a personal account. Here is why, and what the legal options look like.

On this page

Why notOptionsNext step

Why a personal account is not enough

The terms of the big consumer services limit use to personal listening:

  • Spotify grants permission for “personal, non-commercial use” (Spotify End User Agreement, section 3).
  • Apple allows use of its services and content “only for personal, noncommercial purposes” (Apple Media Services Terms, section F).
  • YouTube’s terms say you may listen to content for personal, non-commercial use, and that you may not publicly screen videos or stream music from the service (YouTube Terms of Service).

Playing a personal plan to customers goes beyond those terms.

The legal options

Business music service
Published entry prices start around $15 to $25 per location per month on annual terms: Pandora CloudCover from $16.95, Mood Media from $15.30, Rockbot $25 per zone. Higher tiers cost more. Checked November 12, 2026; see the sources for current prices. A service generally covers its own recorded catalog played in public areas, not live performances, cover artists, DJs or karaoke (Pandora CloudCover, licensing FAQ).
Direct PRO licenses
Venues that play music not supplied by a licensed service, or that host live music, DJs or karaoke, may need licenses from each PRO whose catalog is played (U.S. Copyright Office, “Copyright and the Music Marketplace” (2015)).
Radio or TV, small venue
Certain broadcasts can be exempt in small venues, within equipment limits. See the rules.
