Which business music services include PRO licenses?

Most say they cover the public performance licenses for the music they stream to you. Each one also says, in its own words, where that coverage stops.

What a business music service generally covers

A business music service is not the same as a personal streaming plan. Can I play Spotify in my restaurant or bar? explains why personal accounts are limited to personal use.

The services below describe their coverage as tied to the music they supply. Rockbot, for example, says its licensing “covers public performances of music streamed through Rockbot Music only” (Rockbot, music licensing for business). Soundtrack says its US customers “won't have to obtain public performance licenses from the local PROs, for so long as they use the Service in accordance with this Agreement” (Soundtrack General Terms).

The same pages list what falls outside. The exclusions they name most often are:

  • Live music, DJs and karaoke, named by Soundtrack, Rockbot, Pandora CloudCover and Custom Channels, and DJ use by SiriusXM (sources in the table below). Do I need a license for live music, DJs or karaoke? covers those nights in detail.
  • Music from other sources. Rockbot names “radios, televisions, or personal devices” (Rockbot), and Soundtrack names “another source other than the Service” (Soundtrack General Terms).
  • Admission fees and fitness classes, named by Soundtrack, SiriusXM and Pandora CloudCover. SESAC describes the same limit from its side: a background music provider’s SESAC license “only extends to the music they supply to establishments with no admission, membership or similar charge” (SESAC FAQ).

Some uses vary by service. SiriusXM’s business agreement, for example, allows use “in on-hold messaging on your business telephone system” (SiriusXM Customer Agreement for Music for Business Services). If you need music on hold, check your own service’s terms for it.

Questions to ask before you sign

  • Which PROs does the license cover, by name? Some pages above name all four US PROs, some give examples, and some name none.
  • Which locations and areas? List every address and every area where the service plays: patio, private dining, event space, gym. Soundtrack, for example, asks customers to keep “an accurate and up to date list of your locations” (Soundtrack General Terms).
  • Are live music, DJs, karaoke, trivia, dancing, fitness classes or admission charges excluded? Every service in the table names at least one of these.
  • What if you also play radio, TV or another app? Rockbot, for one, says its licensing does not cover other sources (Rockbot).
  • Is music on hold included? Terms differ from one service to the next.
  • Can you have it in writing? Ask for the clause or a written confirmation that matches your venue. Pandora CloudCover’s own advice: if you are unsure whether you are licensed through a “for Business” account, “you should reach out to them directly and confirm with them” (CloudCover licensing FAQ).
