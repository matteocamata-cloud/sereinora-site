Jukeboxes and TouchTunes: does the bar still need its own music license?

For songs played on the jukebox itself, the big digital jukebox companies say they hold PRO licenses. Their own pages also list the nights and music sources those licenses leave out.

What the law calls a jukebox

The Copyright Act has its own section on jukeboxes. It calls them “coin-operated phonorecord players” and sets four conditions (17 U.S.C. §116(d)). The machine:

  • is used “solely for the performance of nondramatic musical works”, started by coins, currency, tokens or their equivalent;
  • is in an establishment “making no direct or indirect charge for admission”;
  • has a list of all the song titles, posted on the machine or somewhere in the venue where the public can read it;
  • lets the venue’s patrons choose the songs.

The same section defines the “operator” as the person who owns the machine, places it in venues, or controls which songs it offers (17 U.S.C. §116(d)(2)). For TouchTunes, that is a local company: its jukeboxes “are owned and serviced by a local amusement Operator” (TouchTunes for Business, FAQ).

TouchTunes and AMI: what the companies and ASCAP say

On music licensing, TouchTunes’ business FAQ says: “All songs played from the jukebox are fully licensed. With a few exceptions for certain business activities.” It names its PRO licenses: ASCAP, GMR, SESAC and BMI in the United States (TouchTunes for Business, FAQ).

ASCAP confirms the arrangement from its side. It “has licenses in place with major digital jukebox companies, such as TouchTunes and AMI”. A venue using those jukeboxes is “likely covered when you use the jukebox in accordance with the terms of your agreement with the service” (ASCAP Music Licensing FAQs). Note the condition: the terms of your agreement decide what counts.

What the jukebox license leaves out

TouchTunes lists the exceptions itself (TouchTunes for Business, FAQ):

Admission or cover charges
Venues “that charge admission to enter (including cover charges)” must get a separate license directly from the PROs, “regardless of the source”. The legal definition above also requires “no direct or indirect charge for admission”.
Music tied to a paid activity
Venues that use music “in accompaniment with another paid service”. TouchTunes’ examples include karaoke and “disco bowling” nights.
Other music sources
“Our licenses will not cover music played from a source other than our jukebox.” The examples it gives: live music, including DJs, and staff playing music from personal streaming accounts such as Spotify or Apple Music.

So a bar with a jukebox can still need its own PRO licenses for band nights, DJ sets, karaoke or the bartender’s phone. Do I need a license for live music, DJs or karaoke? covers those nights, and Can I play Spotify in my restaurant or bar? covers personal streaming.

Older, non-digital jukeboxes

For years, the Jukebox License Office offered a single license for coin-operated jukeboxes. That has ended. ASCAP’s jukebox page says: “As of 2025, the long-established Jukebox License Office has closed, and we will no longer offer a single PRO license.” It adds: “You will now need to go to ASCAP, BMI and SESAC individually for an annual, coin operated jukebox license” (ASCAP, Jukebox Music License).

ASCAP’s FAQ points to its own jukebox licensing agreement for non-digital jukeboxes (ASCAP Music Licensing FAQs). ASCAP’s bar and restaurant schedule also notes that a separate jukebox fee may apply; see what a license costs.

What to ask your jukebox operator, in writing

  • Which PROs license the music on this jukebox, at this venue: ASCAP, BMI, SESAC, GMR?
  • Can you send a copy of those licenses, or a written statement that names them?
  • Does it change anything on nights when we charge a cover or run karaoke?
  • If the jukebox plays background music when the queue is empty, is that music licensed the same way?
  • For an older coin-operated machine: who holds the ASCAP, BMI and SESAC jukebox licenses now that the Jukebox License Office has closed?

Keep the answers with your other license records. If a PRO letter mentions the jukebox, they are the first thing to look at. Got a letter from BMI, ASCAP, SESAC or GMR? explains the next steps.
