Is my venue exempt from music licensing?

Possibly, but only for certain radio and TV broadcasts, and only within strict limits.

On this page

The ruleNever coveredNext step

The rule

17 U.S.C. §110(5)(B) exempts a business from needing a separate license to play a transmission of a nondramatic musical work that comes from an FCC-licensed radio or TV station (or, for video, a cable system or satellite carrier), if all of these hold (17 U.S.C. §110(5), exemption for certain establishments):

Food and drink venues
Under 3,750 gross square feet (excluding customer parking): no equipment limits.
Other businesses
Under 2,000 gross square feet: no equipment limits.
Larger venues
Audio on no more than 6 loudspeakers, with no more than 4 in any one room. Video on no more than 4 screens, no more than 1 per room, none larger than 55 inches diagonal.
In every case
No direct charge to see or hear the transmission; it is not transmitted further beyond the venue; and the transmission is licensed by the copyright owner.

What it never covers

  • Streaming services, downloads, CDs or vinyl. The statute covers only broadcast and cable or satellite transmissions.
  • Live music, DJs or karaoke.
  • Music you add to social media or marketing videos.

A plain-English summary of the statute for general information. Check the current text, or ask a US attorney, before relying on it. An overview is available from the Congressional Research Service, RS21107.
