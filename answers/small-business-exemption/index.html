Is my venue exempt from music licensing?

Possibly. But only for some radio and TV broadcasts, and only within strict limits.

Rules: United StatesOther countries

The rule

Under 17 U.S.C. §110(5)(B), a business does not need a separate license to play certain radio and TV transmissions of music. The law calls this music a “nondramatic musical work”. The transmission must come from a radio or TV station licensed by the FCC (the Federal Communications Commission). For video, it can also come from a cable system or satellite carrier. The exemption applies only if all of these hold (17 U.S.C. §110(5), exemption for certain establishments):

Food and drink venues
Under 3,750 gross square feet, not counting customer parking. No limits on equipment.
Other businesses
Under 2,000 gross square feet. No limits on equipment.
Larger venues
Sound on no more than 6 loudspeakers, with no more than 4 in any one room. Video on no more than 4 screens, with no more than 1 per room. No screen can be larger than 55 inches, measured diagonally.
In every case
There is no direct charge to see or hear the transmission. The transmission is not sent on beyond the venue. And the copyright owner has licensed the transmission.

What it never covers

  • Streaming services, downloads, CDs or vinyl. The law covers only broadcast, cable and satellite transmissions.
  • Live music, DJs or karaoke.
  • Music you add to social media or marketing videos.

This is a plain-English summary of the law, for general information. Check the current text, or ask a licensed attorney, before you rely on it. You can find an overview from the Congressional Research Service, RS21107.
