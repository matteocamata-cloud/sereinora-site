Can a restaurant or bar play Spotify? Not with a personal account.

The terms of the big consumer services limit use to personal listening:
- Spotify grants permission for "personal, non-commercial use" (Spotify End User Agreement, section 3).

- A business music service. It generally covers its own recorded catalog in public areas, not live performances, cover artists, DJs or karaoke.
- Direct licenses from each PRO whose catalog is played.

How many music uses does one hotel have? Lobby and background music, the restaurant and bar, live entertainment, ballrooms and meeting rooms, gym and spa, music on hold, TVs in rooms and common areas.

ASCAP's hotel license (2022 form, published by AHLA) treats them one by one. A few points worth knowing:

- Music on hold counts. The form's recorded music includes "a music-on-hold telephone system operated by Licensee".
- A restaurant, bar, spa or fitness club run by a third party under a lease or similar agreement is "Third-Party Licensable Activity", outside the hotel license.
- Each PRO licenses only its own catalog. ASCAP's hotel fees "specifically exclude the value" of rights controlled by BMI, SESAC and GMR.
- Event space is described differently by different PROs. SESAC says responsibility for rented or leased areas sits with "the owner/operator of the establishment", while ASCAP's form leaves out certain third-party conferences and business presentations. The license wording and each event contract are where to look.
