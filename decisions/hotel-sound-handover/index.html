01 / Establish the gap

Do you need a new provider, or a clearer decision?

Begin with one recent event, not a general desire for a more premium atmosphere. Perhaps staff repeatedly turn music down during arrivals, then disagree about restoring it for the evening. Record the room, time, consequence and person who intervened. If nobody can name a consequential exception, keep the present arrangement while you observe.

Do not assume a platform lacks the answer. Mood Media markets hospitality music customization; Rockbot describes remote media control, organizational permissions and local updates within central control. Premium studios such as Maison Sonor also offer sonic identity and strategic advisory. Those are supplier descriptions, not independent evidence of performance at your hotel. [1] [2] [3]

Ask your current supplier to demonstrate the specific workflow in your contract: the relevant zone, its schedule, who may override it, how a previous state is restored and what support already covers. If it resolves the exception at an acceptable cost, using it may be the right decision. An additional review needs a residual problem with an accountable owner.

Three questions to take to your existing supplier
  1. Can our duty manager change this room without changing neighbouring rooms?
  2. Can we recover the approved setting after an exceptional event, with clear responsibility?
  3. Does our present service include agreeing the operating rule with staff, or only configuring an agreed rule?

Capabilities and contract scope vary. An unanswered question is a fact to investigate, not proof that another service is needed.

02 / Make the decision usable

One room. One operating card.

For a partially reopened hotel, designs, room uses and staffing may settle at different times. A staged handover is a plausible place for decisions to fall between roles; it does not prove that any particular project has that problem. Use this card with the actual operator and the people who control the system.

Environment and use
Name one physical room and up to three operating moments. Include activities that need speech or quiet.
Current arrangement
Record the existing provider, actual zone boundaries, available staff controls and approved baseline. Mark missing evidence “unknown”.
Decision and exception owners
Name who approves the normal rule, who can intervene on shift and who resolves a disagreement. A designer, integrator and hotel operator may hold different responsibilities.
Permitted comparison
Compare the current state with one reversible adjustment. Agree comparable time windows and a practical observation, such as repeat staff interventions and the reason for them.
Keep, correct or revert
Write the condition for retaining the change, the reason to stop and the exact previous setting to restore. Keep a path to no intervention.
Rights and specialist boundary
Confirm that the intended playback and changes are permitted. Send noise, room treatment or equipment design questions to an appropriately qualified specialist.

In France, the official business guidance describes authorizations and rights obligations for music played in commercial settings. A personal Spotify subscription permits personal, non-commercial listening. Neither a playlist approval nor this card clears venue playback rights; confirm the actual territory, repertoire, service terms and use with the responsible rights adviser. [4] [5]

03 / Illustrative reasoning

Arrival and evening in a shared lounge.

Fictional example. One lounge, one playback zone. These are proposed comparisons, with no measured result or universal sound level.

At arrival, reception reports repeated questions. First check what causes them: music, conversations, equipment or the room itself. If an authorized comparison is appropriate, the duty manager can try a lower existing playback setting during a comparable arrival window and record repeated questions and new staff concerns. If speech is already clear, leave the sound unchanged.

During the evening, the same single zone may serve social seating and people wanting quiet. A different playlist cannot create a second physical zone. Ask the operator which use has priority, whether an existing quiet area is available and whether less added sound is preferable. Do not prescribe new loudspeakers or room treatment without the appropriate specialist evidence.

The useful output is an agreed rule and a way back, rather than a claim that quieter music always works. See Place Study 001 for a fuller fictional comparison of three operating moments.

04 / A next step you can explain

Which decision is still open?

This check stays on the page. It sends and saves nothing. It is a routing aid, not a diagnosis, quotation or ranking of suppliers. A recurring choice alone does not justify a paid review: establish a meaningful consequence first.

For example: repeated staff intervention time, unresolved complaints or conflicting instructions. Keep the actual frequency and consequence available for scope review; an estimate here would not establish financial value.

Choose the closest description above. Unknown information stays unknown.

If the check is unavailable: keep a working setup. Ask your current supplier about configuration; use a qualified specialist for acoustics or equipment. For recurring operating choices, establish a meaningful recurring consequence, the responsible operator and a specific gap after checking existing coverage before considering an independent brief. Without that evidence, observe first.
