How US music licensing works

Music in a US venue is licensed several times over.

Playing recorded or live music in a business is a public performance. In the US, songwriters are paid through performing rights organizations (PROs), mainly ASCAP, BMI, SESAC and GMR. Each licenses only the works of its own members, so one license does not cover another’s songs. A business music service generally covers only the recorded music it supplies, not your band, DJ or karaoke night.

  • ASCAPits members’ songs only
  • BMIits members’ songs only
  • SESACits members’ songs only
  • GMRits members’ songs only
  • and your music servicerecorded background music only

Five separate sources of permission. A gap in any one is a gap in your coverage.

  • $4,500a year on average: what the National Restaurant Association says its members pay to license music.
  • ~200%rise in per-hotel PRO fees from 2021 to 2025 at one unnamed global chain, as cited by the American Hotel & Lodging Association.

Where a song is played without the right license, US law allows statutory damages of $750 to $30,000 per work, and up to $150,000 if a court finds the infringement willful. That range describes the law, not the likely outcome for any venue.

Sources: Bloomberg via Indianapolis Business Journal, “Restaurants, bars say they’re getting squeezed by rising music licensing costs” (July 9, 2025); 17 U.S.C. §504(c), statutory damages; U.S. Copyright Office, “Copyright and the Music Marketplace” (2015); SESAC, “What is a performing rights organization?”; Pandora CloudCover, licensing FAQ; 17 U.S.C. §110(5), exemption for certain establishments.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is this legal advice?

No. Sereinora is not a law firm. The audit is a factual map of what you play, what you hold and where the two do not match. If you have a lawsuit, a demand with a deadline or a lawyer’s letter, speak to a US attorney first. Our report gives them the facts.

Do I have to get on a call?

No. Everything runs by email: a short intake form, the documents you already have, a written report, and one round of follow-up questions.

What do you need from me?

Your music service contract (or its name), any PRO invoices or letters, a list of rooms and live-music nights, the venue’s approximate floor area, and whether you post videos with music.

Can you promise I will save money?

No. The report shows where you may be paying twice and gives you the exact question to ask each provider, in writing, before you cancel anything.

What happens to the documents I send?

They are used only for your audit. We never publish your name or results without your written permission. See the privacy page for details, including deletion on request.

What if the audit finds a gap?

The report states the exposure as a range under US copyright law and lists ten actions in order. It does not predict what a PRO or a court would do.
